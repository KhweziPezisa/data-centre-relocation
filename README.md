# Data Centre Relocation - 108-Device Rack Migration

## Overview
Physical decommissioning, relocation, and recommissioning of 108 network and compute devices for a top-5 pan African banking client's data centre at Samrand, driven by a new BON expansion. Executed on-site by a 4-person team during after-hours maintenance windows between 8 and 29 October 2026, with zero downtime to production services.

## Business Driver
The relocation was required to support a new BON expansion, necessitating the physical repositioning of existing infrastructure to accommodate the expanded footprint.

## Scope
- **Devices relocated:** 108 (nodes, switches, spines, and related infrastructure)
- **Client:** Top-5 pan African banking client (anonymised)
- **Location:** Samrand data centre
- **Duration:** 8 October – 29 October 2026
- **Team:** 4-person team; full scope of work (decommissioning, relocation, patching, labelling) performed collaboratively across all team members, including myself

## Work Performed

### 1. Device Decommissioning
- Safely powered down devices (nodes, switches, spines) following change control procedures
- Unplugged and labelled cables prior to disconnection
- Detached devices from existing racks

### 2. Relocation
- Physically transported devices to new rack locations
- Re-mounted and secured devices in new racks per rack elevation plans

### 3. Cabling & Patching
- Patched fibre cabling, including SFP/QSFP transceiver terminations where required
- Patched RJ45 (copper) cabling, including SFP-based connections where applicable
- Reconnected power cabling (PDUs/power feeds)

### 4. Cable Management & Labelling
- Dressed and routed cables per best-practice cable management standards
- Labelled all relevant cabling for traceability and future maintenance

## Standards & Best Practices
- TIA-942 data centre infrastructure standard
- ANSI/BICSI cabling standards
- Change executed after hours, within the client's approved maintenance window

## Tools & Equipment
- Hand tools: star screwdrivers, flatheads, pliers
- Labelling: Brother Labeling Machine LAB194
- Cable management: velcro bands
- Transceivers: Huawei SFP28-25GBASE-T 25G Copper Transceiver Module; Huawei QSFP-100G-SR4 100GBASE-SR4 QSFP28 Optical Transceiver; Huawei QSFP-40G-SR4 40GBASE-SR4 QSFP+ Optical Transceiver; Huawei OMXD30000 10GBASE-SR SFP+ Optical Transceiver; Huawei SFP-25G-SR 25GBASE-SR SFP28 Optical Transceiver
- Fibre patch cables: OM3 multi-mode 12-fiber MPO-to-MPO patch cable, OM4 LC duplex fiber optic patch cord

## Outcomes
- 108 devices successfully relocated and recommissioned with zero downtime within the planned maintenance window

## Key Learnings
- Gained hands-on data centre operations experience at scale, moving beyond cloud/software infrastructure into the physical layer: rack-and-stack, structured cabling, and transceiver handling across copper and fibre media types
- Applied formal standards (TIA-942, ANSI/BICSI) consistently across 108 devices within tight after-hours maintenance windows, where mistakes carry real cost on live banking infrastructure
- Built operational discipline around zero-downtime execution, including sequencing, verification, and rollback readiness during physical changes to production network gear
- Developed practical competence with mixed-media cabling, including SFP, QSFP, and QSFP28 transceiver types across speeds from 10G to 100G, and matching the correct fibre/copper patch cable to each
- Strengthened team coordination skills by executing a large, multi-person physical change within a fixed overnight window, requiring task sequencing, communication, and division of labour under time pressure
- Reinforced the value of traceability and documentation habits, through consistent cable labelling and management practices that support long-term maintainability
